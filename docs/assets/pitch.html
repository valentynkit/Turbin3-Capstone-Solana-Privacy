Silent Confidential Payments

Pay anyone on Solana so the chain shows neither who received it nor how much. An auditor key keeps it compliant.

The problem: two half-solutions that don't fit together

Confidential balances (Token-2022)

Amount hidden. Recipient public. Live on mainnet since mid-2026, usage ≈ 0, Phantom doesn't support it.

Stealth addresses (sRFC-42)

Recipient hidden (fresh address per payment). Amount public. Reference impl dormant, devnet only.

Why nobody combined them

To set up a confidential account you must prove you hold its encryption key and sign as the owner. A sender paying a stealth address holds neither. So the recipient has to be online and set up every account by hand, which defeats the whole point of a reusable address.

What is new

  • Sender-side setup of a confidential account for an offline recipient, by deriving the ElGamal key from the shared secret. zk-sdk already ships the raw-material key derivation and a PDA helper; nobody has wired them to a stealth scheme.
  • Raw-scalar Ed25519 verified inside a program to authorise a PDA-owned account. The existing stealth reference only signs at transaction level.
  • First system anywhere hiding both counterparty and amount, with an audit path. Umbra/Fluidkey: amounts public. Rings/Arcium/MagicBlock: different trust models, not Token-2022 native.

Who it's for · and who it's not

For

Grant programs and DAOs paying contributors · companies paying contractors and suppliers in stablecoins · donors and creators who don't want their income graph public.

Not for

Payroll. The employer already knows the employee, and ~5 transactions per payment is wrong for hundreds of payments twice a month.

Limitations we state up front

  • Sweep re-links. Consolidating many one-time accounts into one clusters them. Mitigation: spend from them directly or sweep into a fresh one.
  • Sender keeps a decryption key for that one account. So accounts are strictly one-time, swept and closed promptly.
  • Cost. ~5 txs and ~223k CU of proof verification per transfer; ~0.013 SOL locked until close. One-off payments only.
  • Needs a cryptographer's eyes on domain separation before code. First thing we do.

Why this project

The hard part is cryptography and program design, not distribution. It is useful with zero partners, built on the primitives the Foundation is pushing, and fills a gap their own samples leave open (no PDA-owner example anywhere). Honest limitations are the red-team appendix.
