HOW IT WORKS · DRAFT · 2026-09-12

A confidential payout rail on Solana

Pay many people with hidden amounts. Recipients can stay unlinkable. The payer stays visible. The money stays an ordinary token. An auditor can still read the numbers. No pool, nothing to trust beyond Solana.

1. The problem

Every payout on Solana is public: who was paid, and how much. That is the stated reason companies keep payroll, grants, and supplier payments off-chain: "our books would be public."

Solana already has a fix for the amount, and a draft fix for the recipient. Neither is usable for real payout flows, and they were never joined.

Confidential balances (Token-2022)

Amount: hidden. Recipient: a fixed public address, so the payment pattern is visible. Each transfer needs three proofs and several transactions, and there is no tooling to run a payout to fifty people.
Live on mainnet. Almost nobody uses it.

Stealth addresses (sRFC-42)

Recipient: hidden, fresh address per payment. Amount: public. And it cannot be combined with confidential balances: setting up a confidential account needs the owner's signature and encryption key, which a payer sending to a fresh address does not have.
Draft, devnet reference.

2. What we build

The rail

A payout run: N recipients, hidden amounts, proofs generated in batch on the payer's machine, fees handled, rent reclaimed, one auditor path. This is where most of the engineering is.

The primitive

For recipients who want to be unlinkable: a fresh, program-owned account per payment that the payer can set up alone, because its encryption key is derived from the secret both sides share. Nobody else has this.
Two recipient modes, chosen per recipient in the same run. Plain: the recipient has a confidential account already; we transfer to it. Amount hidden, address fixed. Stealth: the recipient has published one reusable address; each payment lands in a fresh account only they can find. Amount and recipient hidden.

4. The trick behind stealth mode

Token-2022 requires the account owner to sign the setup and prove they hold the encryption key. A payer sending to a fresh address has neither. Two changes remove the wall:

The fresh account is owned by a program

A program can sign for accounts it owns, so it does the setup on the payer's behalf.

The encryption key comes from the shared secret

The stealth handshake gives payer and recipient a secret nobody else has (ECDH). The account's encryption key is derived from it. The payer can make the proof; the recipient can later decrypt.

5. One payout run, step by step

1
Recipients publish, once. Stealth recipients put one reusable address in the address book. Plain recipients just have a confidential account.
2
Payer prepares the run. For each stealth recipient: derive a fresh address P, a view tag, and an encryption key. For everyone: generate the transfer proofs, in batch, locally.
3
Payer executes. Plain recipients get a confidential transfer. Stealth recipients get a program-owned account created and configured, then funded, plus a little SOL on P for their future fees. Amounts are encrypted from the first block. Proof accounts are closed as they go; rent comes back.
4
Stealth recipients find their money. Filter fresh accounts by view tag (255 of 256 discarded instantly), try the handshake on the rest, decrypt the balance.
5
Sweep. The recipient signs a transaction with P and pays the fee from P, so their own wallet never appears. The program checks the signature and moves the funds, still encrypted, into a fresh account of the recipient's own. The old one is closed, rent returned.
6
Audit, if required. The mint's auditor key reads every amount on that mint. Or either party discloses the key for one payment. Nobody gets spend access.

8. Limits we state up front

Payer is visible

Deliberate. It keeps this out of mixer territory. Need payer privacy? Use a pool.

Consolidation links

Sweeping many fresh accounts into one main account clusters them. Rule: sweep into a fresh account, spend from there.

Payer holds that one account's key

Can read that balance until close. Hence one-time accounts, and never spending directly from a payer-funded account.

Cost

Stealth mode is about twice a plain confidential transfer; roughly ten transactions and 0.013 SOL locked until close. Batching amortises the proof work, not the transaction count.

Adoption we don't control

No wallet shows confidential balances. Issuers choose whether to set an auditor key. The rail is useful only as far as confidential balances are.

Review pending

Two keys from one shared secret must be domain-separated. Recipe written, outside reviewer not yet.

9. Glossary

Confidential balancea Token-2022 account whose balance and transfer amounts are encrypted; proofs show the math is right without revealing numbers
Stealth addressa fresh address per payment, derived from one reusable published address, that only the recipient can recognise and spend from
ECDH shared secreta secret two parties can each compute from their own private key and the other's public key, and nobody else can
View tagone byte of the shared secret hash, published so the recipient can skip 255 of 256 accounts cheaply
Program-owned account (PDA)an account whose owner is a program, not a keypair; the program can sign for it
Auditor keya key set on the mint by its issuer that can decrypt every amount on that mint, read only
Poola program holding everyone's funds as encrypted notes so transfers inside it can't be traced; privacy comes from the crowd
